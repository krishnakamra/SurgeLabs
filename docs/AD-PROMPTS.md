# Making the price ad yourself

Prompts for ChatGPT (or any image model), plus the exact text to lay over
them. Written for the offer we agreed: 1,000 business cards $99 · 500 flyers
from $120 · printed tees from $12 · websites from $399.

---

## The one rule: never let the model write the prices

Image models garble numerals and short words. Ask for "$99" and you get
"$9g", "$099", "buisness cards". An ad with a wrong price on it is a price
you did not mean to offer, and the customer who screenshots it is entitled
to hold you to it.

So the split is:

- **ChatGPT makes the photograph.** No text in the image at all.
- **You add the text in Canva** (or Photoshop, or `npm run ad:photo`).

Every prompt below ends with "no text, no words, no lettering" for that
reason. Keep that line in.

---

## A note on what these are

These are staged product photographs generated by a model. They are not
photographs of jobs Surge Labs ran. Use them the way a supplier uses a
catalogue shot — to show what a product *is* — and don't caption them as
past work or as a specific client's job. The moment you have real photos
from your own floor, use those instead: they will beat every prompt on this
page, and they are actually yours.

> sharp and in focus in the foreground, three more cards fanned out beside the
> stack, all cards completely blank with a plain deep navy and white colour
> block design and no lettering. A paper guillotine and stacked cartons
> slightly out of focus behind. Warm overhead shop lighting mixed with
> daylight from a bay door, real working environment, mild wear on the table.
> Shot on a 50mm lens at f/2.8, slight grain. No text, no words, no numbers,
> no logos, no lettering anywhere.

goes.

## 6. Backdrop, if you want the products smaller and the type bigger

> Wide interior of a small modern print and signage workshop in an industrial

> a multi-head embroidery machine at the back, boxes and rolls of vinyl on

## Setting it in Canva

1. New design → **1080 × 1080** for Instagram and Facebook feed,
   **1080 × 1920** for a story, **1200 × 628** for a link ad or email header.
   Make the square one first and resize after.
2. Background: `#0A0A0B`. Not pure black — it prints and compresses better.
3. Your logo top left. Use the real file from `public/brand/` — never let a
   model draw it.
4. Headline in **Montserrat ExtraBold**, white `#EFEDE8`.
5. Prices in **Montserrat ExtraBold** in lime `#CCFF00`. That green on that
   black is the highest contrast pair in the brand, which is why the prices
   get it and nothing else does.
6. Specs in Montserrat Regular, grey `#A3A29E`, about half the price size.
7. Phone number bottom right, big enough to read on a phone at arm's length.
   That is the whole point of the ad.

**Then proofread every digit against the list above before you send it.**

## Or let the repo render it

`npm run ad` makes the typographic version — no photos, prices read straight
out of `content/packages.ts`, so it cannot disagree with the site.

`npm run ad:photo` makes this photo version. It needs the product shots on
disk first:

    node scripts/fetch-media.mjs      # downloads them into public/media/stills
    npm run ad:photo                  # writes ads/surge-labs-photo-*.jpg

To use your own photographs instead, drop four files named `g-cards-16pt`,
`g-print-flyers`, `g-wear-tees` and `panel-web` (`.jpg` or `.png`) in a
folder and run:

    PHOTO_DIR=/path/to/your/photos npm run ad:photo

Both scripts refuse to run if an offer has been renamed or removed from
`content/packages.ts`, so a stale price can't slip into an ad.
